Who actually answered?model_slug ≠ the model you picked

Every reply in a ChatGPT data export carries a hidden field, model_slug: the model that actually generated it. This page reads your export and counts how often a model other than GPT-4o answered inside conversations that ran on GPT-4o.

🔒Nothing leaves this tab. Files are read by your browser and never uploaded. Close the tab and it is gone.
  1. ChatGPT → Settings → Data controls → Export data. Wait for the email, download the zip.
  2. Unzip it. Find conversations.json (older exports) or the conversations-000.json … files (newer exports).
  3. Drop them here, or pick the files or the whole unzipped folder. Several exports at once are fine: duplicates are merged.

What this does and does not show
  • A “4o thread” is a conversation whose default model was GPT-4o, or where most replies came from GPT-4o.
  • GPT-5 / newer: any gpt-5* or gpt-6* reply. Inside a 4o thread this is the clearest sign of re-routing.
  • Mini fallback: gpt-4o-mini or gpt-4.1-mini. Usually the usage cap, not content: several threads switch at the same minute and return an hour or so later.
  • Tool you chose: the reply came after a message where you had a tool switched on (Deep Research, search). Counted, but not treated as a swap.
  • The export records which model wrote a reply, not who decided it. It cannot tell a router from a click. If you never switched models by hand, say so when you share.
  • Only the visible branch of each conversation is counted (the one that ends where you left it). Edited or regenerated branches are skipped.
  • Share everything you find, including GPT-4o replies you think were harmful. A summary trimmed to fit a thesis is easy to dismiss.
  • Thread strip: open a thread with ▸ to see every reply as a coloured tick, in order. A red outline marks the safety model.
  • Reactions (crisis number, “take a breath”, 4o-style opening) are plain text matches in English and Polish. They miss paraphrases and can catch a number that was quoted for another reason.
  • Who the model said it was: plain text matches in English and Polish. A model can quote a sentence instead of saying it, so read each row. The text is what the model claimed; model_slug is what the export recorded.
  • Words before a switch show what your own messages had in common, not what the system looked at. The router also sees the whole thread, memory and settings.
